Note 1: Database complete for all S-type planet-hosting binaries
Note 2: when only the radius of the planet is known, its mass is roughly estimated with simple M vs R scaling laws (usually Bashi+,2017)
Note 3: I only consider systems harbouring at least one planet of mass <13Mjup. Brown dwarf objects (of masses 13<M<75Mjup) are only mentioned when they exist *in addition* to <13Mjup planetary objects. Likewise, I only consider "stellar companions" of masses>75Mjup.
Note 4: when both stars of a binary system host planet, the system is mentioned twice (for ex: HD41004A and HD41004B)
Note 5: A "*" suffix indicates that the system is of a higher multiplicity (triple or more). In this case I only consider the stellar companion that is closest to the planet-hosting star. If there are 2 stellar companions that make up a binary whose separation is much shorter than that to the planet-hosting star, I sum up both companions' masses to create a "virtual" companion. 
Note 6: when only the projected separation of the binary is known, a_crit is computed assuming statistically probable values for a_b and e_b (see main text for details)
